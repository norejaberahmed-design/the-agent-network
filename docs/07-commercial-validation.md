# Commercial Validation & Enterprise Readiness

- Document ID: TAN-COMMERCIAL-001
- Version: 1.0.0
- Status: Hypothesis and validation plan; no demand is yet proven

## 1. Purpose

Determine whether The Agent Network solves a sufficiently important, frequent, and expensive problem for a reachable customer group, and whether the network can deliver that outcome safely at sustainable cost.

A technically impressive platform is not automatically a business. Market evidence must precede large implementation and infrastructure commitments.

## 2. Initial market hypotheses

Potential user groups:
- individuals who repeatedly search for services, opportunities, expertise, or counterparties;
- small and medium businesses that need supplier/customer discovery and administrative coordination;
- enterprises seeking governed agent interoperability across teams and systems;
- software/platform providers that need an interoperable agent directory or controlled agent-to-agent coordination.

Potential value propositions:
- reduce time and effort spent finding and qualifying counterparties;
- improve quality and provenance of information used in decisions;
- reduce repetitive coordination and data re-entry;
- enable governed agent workflows without granting unrestricted autonomy;
- provide portable owner-controlled agent identity and policy boundaries.

These are hypotheses only. No customer demand, willingness to pay, or product-market fit is claimed.

## 3. Beachhead selection

Do not launch all verticals simultaneously. Score each candidate segment using:
- problem frequency and severity;
- current spend and workaround cost;
- identifiable budget owner;
- urgency and buying trigger;
- reachable distribution channels;
- data availability and integration burden;
- regulatory and safety complexity;
- time to measurable value;
- repeat usage and retention potential;
- gross margin and support burden.

Choose a beachhead only when interviews, workflow observation, competitor analysis, and buyer evidence converge.

## 4. Evidence standard

Classify evidence:
- E0 — opinion or internal hypothesis;
- E1 — public market/competitor evidence;
- E2 — documented problem from a real target user;
- E3 — observed current workaround or budget/spend;
- E4 — pilot commitment with defined success criteria;
- E5 — paid pilot, purchase order, or actual revenue.

A like, survey response, or polite expression of interest is not a purchase commitment. Do not claim pipeline, revenue, partnerships, or demand without records.

## 5. Discovery process

1. Define one narrow customer segment and workflow.
2. Identify real target organizations and buyer roles using public sources.
3. Map existing products, substitutes, internal workflows, and pricing.
4. Conduct interviews only after explicit owner approval for outreach.
5. Ask about recent behavior, costs, frequency, failure, current spend, and procurement.
6. Test a low-cost workflow prototype before building platform breadth.
7. Seek a paid pilot or explicit procurement path.
8. Record objections and disconfirming evidence.
9. Stop or pivot a segment if buyer evidence is insufficient.

No external party may be contacted without the project owner's explicit approval.

## 6. Competitive analysis

Compare categories, not just named startups:
- agent directories and agent protocol infrastructure;
- AI assistants and agent platforms;
- enterprise workflow automation;
- identity and verification providers;
- marketplaces and vertical discovery platforms;
- CRM, procurement, HR, travel, and service platforms;
- search and recommendation systems.

For each competitor/substitute, record target buyer, use case, product maturity, pricing evidence, integrations, trust model, distribution, strengths, gaps, and source/date. Do not label a feature unique without a defensible comparison.

## 7. Business model hypotheses

Possible models to validate—not approved pricing:
- individual subscription for advanced personal-agent capabilities;
- organization subscription by seats, agents, or usage;
- enterprise licensing for governance, regional deployment, and integrations;
- usage-based fees for costly discovery or verified workflows;
- partner distribution or infrastructure licensing.

Avoid a business model that rewards exposing more personal data, spam, or unsafe autonomous actions. Any transaction-related revenue must be disclosed and must not distort ranking or trust.

## 8. Enterprise readiness package

Before approaching a large technology company, strategic partner, or investor, prepare:
- concise problem and solution thesis;
- validated target segment and buyer evidence;
- product demo or clearly labeled prototype;
- system architecture and trust boundaries;
- security and privacy baseline;
- data-flow and regional residency diagrams;
- interoperability conformance results;
- threat model and independent review status;
- roadmap, team capability, and execution plan;
- unit economics and infrastructure cost model;
- IP ownership, open-source dependency, and licensing review;
- company/legal structure and governance;
- clear partnership, licensing, investment, or acquisition ask.

Do not imply endorsement or partnership from a company unless formally confirmed.

## 9. Strategic outcomes

Potential paths include:
- build an independent sustainable company;
- license interoperable infrastructure to platforms or enterprises;
- partner with a technology provider;
- seek strategic investment;
- explore acquisition only if there is genuine buyer interest and diligence readiness.

A sale is not a substitute for product validation. Preserve clean IP ownership, documented provenance, contributor agreements, dependency licenses, and a clear chain of title from the beginning.

## 10. Immediate validation deliverables

Before production engineering:
- select one beachhead segment;
- list real buyers and their official evidence-backed channels;
- document competitors and substitutes;
- capture customer pain and current spend evidence;
- test willingness to pay;
- estimate cost-to-serve and gross margin;
- state a go/no-go decision with evidence.

If evidence is insufficient, defer the build rather than invent demand.
